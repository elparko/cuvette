# Comparing two groups

1. Read the data. Find the events, the person-time or the number at risk, and the group of each row.
2. Ask which group is group A (the exposed or treated group) and which is group B (the reference).
3. Ask if the outcome is a rate (events per person-time) or a risk (events among persons at risk). Use person-time for a rate.
4. Ask for the interval method of the rate ratio, the rate multiplier and the confidence level. Ask for the follow-up time of the risks.
5. Run compare_rates. Read the rate ratio, the rate difference, the risk ratio, the risk difference and the odds ratio.
6. For the number needed to treat, read the direction. A positive risk difference gives a number needed to harm.
7. If the interval of the risk difference includes 0, report the interval of the number needed as going through infinity.
8. Ask for the exposure prevalence of the population before you report the population attributable fraction.
9. Report the numerator and the denominator of each group, so the reader can check the ratio.
10. Do not call the attributable fraction a share of caused cases without the assumption of a causal effect.
